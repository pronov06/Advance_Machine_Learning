Likely questions — and honest answers

Why JavaScript and not Python?It runs anywhere with no install, and writing each algorithm from scratch (DQN, GCN, SPARQL, Apriori, a CNN with hand-coded back-prop) shows we understand them. The maths is identical to the notebooks.
Is this the same data as the notebooks?Karate Club and the textbook FP-growth example are real. Olivetti faces, Iris and digits need downloads, so they are replaced by generated stand-ins (a 12-person synthetic face gallery, Iris-like samples drawn from the real class means and spreads). Each chapter says so.
Does DQN always learn here?No — and that is realistic. The default seed reaches ≈100 steps; other seeds can take longer or stall. The ablation (no replay, no target net) fails on every seed I tried, which is the point.
Did deeper GNNs over-smooth?Not dramatically on a 34-node graph: ≈97 % up to 6 layers, ≈94 % at 8. What the page shows is the cause — the receptive field grows until it covers the whole graph; the damage appears on larger graphs and with fewer labels.
How do you know back-prop is correct?Gradient checks against finite differences: ≈1e-10 error on the walkthrough network (T14 prints it live); the same check passes for networks with BatchNorm and Dropout.
How do you know Apriori and FP-Growth are right?They match an exhaustive search on 900 random databases, and T11/T12 show the equality check on screen. Subgraph enumeration was checked the same way on 1,200 random graphs.
Why is the bias–variance demo regression, not classification?Squared loss has a clean additive decomposition; the notebook’s 0-1-loss version is less clean. The page measures it by refitting on 40 fresh datasets.

If something goes wrong

  • A page looks stuck → press Reset on that page, or reload (everything is deterministic from its seed).
  • Training too slow on the projector laptop → lower train speed, or train before you start talking.
  • A slider “did nothing” → many need Apply & reset or Reset (the label says “needs reset”).
  • Short on time → drop T7 and T15; the T2 → T4 → T13 sequence tells the whole story in four minutes.
